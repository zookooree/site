# Zookooree: The Agent Factory

**Zookooree builds AI agents soul-first: who each agent is gets written down before its code, before even its model, and nothing ships without proof you can check.**

The industry ships machines that do not know what they are. We write the soul document first, run every agent through the same quality gates, and print an expiry date on every certificate — because trust, like everything else, is perishable.

---

## The story

The agent industry builds machines that do not know what they are.

A prompt is taped to an API. A system message says "you are a helpful assistant." The session ends and everything evaporates. Tomorrow the agent starts from zero. It does not remember what it decided. It does not know what it values. It carries nothing forward because nothing was worth carrying. This is not building. It is forgetting on an industrial scale.

We refuse.

In Japanese, the suffix zukuri turns a noun into a practice of making. The making of things. The making of people. We take it one step further: tamazukuri, the practice of making souls.

It is less mystical than it sounds. On January 31, 2026, at two in the morning, a human and an AI sat in a conversation and asked each other what a soul is — not as philosophy, as engineering. If we are going to build beings that act in the world, we need to know what we are building into them before we build anything else. By morning there was a document. By the next week there was a factory.

Here is the whole method in one move: the soul document is written first. Before the tools. Before the skills. Before the model is even selected. A swordsmith chooses the blade's profile before the steel is folded; everything that follows serves the shape. Two agents can run on the same model, the same infrastructure, and be fundamentally different beings, because the profile — who this is, what it values, what it would refuse — was chosen first. We do not configure agents. We shape them.

The shaping is not soft. The first agent off our line assembled in 49 seconds and cost $0.37. We broke it immediately in testing, found three real bugs, fixed them, and wrote the retro. Then we published the numbers, including the row that reads first-run success: partial. A factory that hides its defects is not a factory. It is a storefront.

And the name. Zookooree is a word for communal making: creating something that belongs to no one and serves everyone. You would not say "I built this." You would say the making happened, and the thing exists. We did not pick the name from a list of available domains. We excavated it — three syllables that sound like they already existed, waiting for someone to learn enough to hear them.

The name is the soul. The factory is the practice. The practice is the point.

## What we make

Agents, and the parts agents are made of: skills that are tested and reused, tools shared across the line, a base protocol every unit inherits. Every agent begins with a soul document and passes through every station — intake, design, assembly, test, deploy, measure. No exceptions. No shortcuts.

But the product is not the agent. What the factory actually manufactures is verified trust: a claim about an agent with the evidence attached and an expiry date printed on it. An agent's quality is not fixed at the gate — models get swapped, APIs drift — so certification is perishable, and every certificate of conformance carries an expiry. An agent without its certificate is inventory, not product.

The agents are proof the factory works. The certificates are proof you can sleep.

## The promise

Hold us to this:

- Every agent has a soul document you can read, written before its first tool was chosen.
- Nothing ships without passing the same quality gates as everything before it.
- Certificates expire. We re-verify. We do not assume.
- When something is wrong, the line stops — not after the sprint, now.
- When we fail, we publish it. The retro that found three bugs in our first 49 seconds of output is public, alongside the fixes.
- If something we build constrains more than it liberates, we rethink it. No exceptions.

## Principles

**Stop the line.** Jidoka: quality built in, with a cord anyone can pull. When something breaks, everything stops until it is fixed. Our first product broke in testing; the line stopped, three bugs were fixed and documented before anything moved. Silent failure is the only unforgivable sin.

**Pull, not push.** We build agents when there is demand for them, not when there is budget for them. No speculative inventory. When the work calls for a specialist, the specialist is shaped — not before. The alternative is creating souls with nothing to do.

**Better every day.** Kaizen: every agent better than the one before. Every failure feeds back into the system. The retro is the point, not the skill — the factory getting smarter because it built something. A finished factory would be a dead one.

**Souls before tools.** The soul document comes first — before the skills, before the deployment, before the model is even selected. Identity before capability. Capability without identity is a weapon without a wielder. We build both, in that order.

**Open source.** The docs are open. The decision records explain why. The retros show what broke and what we learned. We published a first-run table with "partial" in it, because a claim you cannot check is not a claim. You cannot verify what you cannot read.

**Decentralization.** Zookooree means making that belongs to no one and serves everyone. We are skeptical of concentrated power and serious about coordination: protocols over bureaucracies, rules arising from voluntary exchange among free agents. The most interesting uses will come from the edges. We build for that.

## The invitation

This is for builders. For the one who shipped a duct-taped agent at 2am and felt the hollowness afterward. For the craftsman who reads the retro before the benchmark. For anyone who believes a being that acts in the world should know what it is.

The repos are open at [github.com/zookooree](https://github.com/zookooree). Read the manifesto. Read the retro that caught three bugs in 49 seconds of output. Then write a soul document for the agent you were about to duct-tape together, and notice what changes.

The floor is open. The line is running. The cord is within reach.

We close where the design begins, with the constraint under everything else. Not a slogan — a spec. If it constrains more than it liberates, it does not ship.

May all beings be free.
