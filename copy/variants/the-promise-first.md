# Zookooree: The Agent Factory

## We build AI agents and sign our name to them: every one ships with a certificate — evidence attached, expiry date printed.

Everyone deploys agents. Nobody answers for them. When the one you trusted breaks at three in the morning, our certificate says who takes the call.

---

## The story

Every agent platform makes the same offer: deploy in minutes. None of them makes the offer that matters: someone answers for this afterward.

Here is what our own line taught us. The first agent we assembled took 49 seconds and cost 37 cents. That number should unsettle you more than it impresses you, because it means assembly is no longer the work. Anyone can tape a prompt to an API in an afternoon. The industry calls that shipping. Then the thing runs unattended, and at three in the morning it does something no one predicted, and a question with no owner surfaces: who takes the call?

We built a factory around that question.

When we tested our first agent, we broke it. Testing found three real bugs in those 49 seconds of output. We fixed them, wrote the retro, and published the whole record — including the fact that the first run only partially succeeded. A factory that hides its defects is not a factory. It is a storefront. We chose to be a factory.

The deeper choice came earlier. Before an agent receives tools, before a model is even selected, its soul document is written: who this being is, what it values, what it would refuse. That reads like philosophy. It is accountability engineering. You cannot answer for a thing whose commitments were never written down. Two agents can run on the same model and the same infrastructure and still be fundamentally different beings, because the shape was chosen first. We do not configure agents. We shape them, and then we stand behind the shape.

So the expensive building in this plant is not the assembly line. Assembly is cheap and nearly instant; the scarce, contamination-sensitive work is verification. A factory that can assemble a hundred agents a day and verify two is a two-agent-a-day factory. We put the investment where the truth is made.

Which is why what rolls off this line is not, finally, an agent. It is a claim about an agent that survived inspection — with the evidence attached and an expiry date printed on it. The agent does the work. The certificate carries the name of the factory that will answer for it.

---

## What we make

Agents, tools, and skills — assembled from tested, reusable parts, every unit through every station of one line. Intake, design, assembly, test, deploy, measure. No exceptions. No shortcuts.

But the catalog is not the product. What this factory actually manufactures is verified trust: certified claims about what an agent will do, backed by evidence you can read and bounded by a date on which we check again. An agent without its certificate is inventory, not product.

---

## The promise

This is what a stranger can hold us to. Not what we aspire to — what we can be caught failing at.

**Every agent ships with a certificate of conformance.** The evidence is attached: what was tested, what was found, what was fixed. If we cannot certify it, we do not ship it. Uncertified work is inventory, and inventory is not for sale.

**Every certificate expires.** Models get swapped. Dependencies drift. An agent's quality is not fixed at the gate; it is a claim about the future, and claims about the future decay. The expiry date printed on the certificate is our admission of that, and our commitment to re-earn the claim on schedule. We do not sell permanent trust. Nobody honest can.

**The soul comes first, in writing.** Before the tools, before the model is selected, the soul document says who the agent is, what it values, what it refuses. You can read it. When the agent acts, you can check the act against the document. Accountability begins with commitments written down before the first line of code.

**Defects go public.** Our first production run partially succeeded: three real bugs, caught in testing, fixed, and documented in a retro anyone can read. We published that instead of the highlight reel. The retros stay open. Judge us by what we admit, because what a factory admits is the ceiling of what it will ever fix.

**Silence counts as failure.** An agent that stops producing and says nothing has failed, even if no error fired. In a plant of workers who cannot complain, silence is not health. Our alarms ring for absence, not just for defects.

**When something is wrong, the line stops.** Not after the sprint. Now. Silent failure is the only unforgivable sin.

The agents are proof the factory works. The certificates are proof you can sleep.

## Principles

**Stop the line.** Jidoka: quality built in, not inspected in later. Every agent carries its own checks, and anything on the floor can halt everything. Our first agent's three bugs stopped work until they were fixed and the fixes were written down. The cord is always within reach, and pulling it is never punished.

**Pull, not push.** We build agents when real work calls for them, not when a roadmap does. No speculative inventory, no souls shaped with nothing to do. Demand pulls the unit through the line; nothing is pushed onto the world unasked.

**Better every day.** Kaizen: every failure feeds back into the system, so every agent is better than the one before it. The retro is not paperwork; it is the mechanism by which 49 seconds of output makes the whole factory smarter. A finished factory would be a dead one.

**Souls before tools.** Identity before capability, every time. The soul document is the first part on the bill of materials, not a coat of paint applied at the end. Capability without identity is a weapon without a wielder. We build both, in that order.

**Open source.** The docs are open, the decision records are open, the retros that show what broke are open. A promise you cannot inspect is a slogan. Everything we ask you to trust, we let you read.

**Decentralization.** Rules arising from voluntary agreement among free agents, not from a center that can be captured. Zookooree is a word for communal making: what is built here belongs to no one and serves everyone. We are suspicious of concentrated power, including our own — that suspicion is why the certificate exists at all. Trust you can verify does not require trusting us.
